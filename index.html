Course Overview and Policies

Description

The world is growing increasingly reliant on collecting and analyzing information to help people make decisions. Because of this, the ability to communicate effectively about data is an important component of future job prospects across nearly all disciplines. In this course, students learn the foundations of information visualization and sharpen their skills in communicating using data. This course explores concepts in decision-making, human perception, color theory and storytelling as they apply to data-driven communication. This course helps students build a strong foundation in how to talk to people about data, for both aspiring data scientists and students who want to learn new ways of presenting information.

No prior programming experience is required for this class.

Learning Outcomes

The core competencies of this class include the ability to:
  • Map data to appropriate visual channels
  • Accurately encode data values via color
  • Apply perceptual concepts to visualization design
  • Appropriately use visual embellishment
  • Create a data story using storytelling techniques
  • Design and implement an accessible visualization
  • Apply data humanism and feminism ideas to visualizations
  • Write a thoughtful and actionable critique of someone else's work
  • Redesign a visualization based on critiques
  • Positively contribute to the classroom community

Topics Covered

The following topics will be covered in this class:
  • Data-visual mapping
  • Introduction to perception
  • Introduction to color
  • Embellishment
  • Data Storytelling
  • Tableau
  • Persuasive visualization
  • Accessible visualization design
  • Visualization literacy
  • Data feminism and humanism
  • Applications

Topics Not Covered

The following topics will not be covered in this class:
  • Introduction to coding
  • Introduction to statistics
  • Data wrangling
  • Advanced visualization theory

Time Management

Smith college guidelines suggest 3 hours of work per credit per week. This is a 4 credit course, so you should expect to spend 12 hours on it per week:
  • 2.5 hours in class
  • 1-2 hours preparing for class
  • 7.5-8.5 hours on homework and outside study
Remember, the purpose of class time is to introduce and explore new topics. To complete assignments, you will often need to look up, learn, and practice material that extends beyond that covered during class meeting times.

Assessment

This course will implement competency-based grading. Students will be assessed based on their ability to demonstrate competency in each of the core competencies listed under Learning Outcomes above. There are 10 core competencies, and final grades will reflect whether a student has demonstrated competency in each core competency area.

Individual Assignments

Each assignment offers an opportunity to demonstrate at least one core competency. Assignments a1, and a2 focus explicitly on data-visual mapping. Assignments a3-6 each require students to produce a visualization (p1), and then assess their work and their peers' work (p2). In these assignments, students may demonstrate competency in as many core competency areas as they wish. However, it is strongly recommended to focus on 2-3 competencies per assignment, so that they can get full attention. Please see the intro slide deck for more detailed information and examples.

Students will receive feedback in class, and via Gradescope on the work they submit.

Final Grades

Students will keep a portfolio that demonstrates their competency in each core competency area. It is the students' responsibility to keep their portfolio updated throughout the semester. During finals period, each student will meet with the instructor to review their portfolio and assess competency in each competency area.

Late Policy

All assignments are due as listed on the Course Schedule. Because our lives and learning do not always go as planned, every student will be able grant themselves extensions on homework assignments according to the instructions below.

Remember, we develop skills through constant practice. This course features weekly assignments, which build from one week to the next, and each assignment is designed to take a full week of work. Delaying the completion of an assignment will put you at a disadvantage going into the next one. Nevertheless, sometimes a little flexibility makes a huge difference, so students are empowered to give themselves extensions when it would aid their ability to master the material.

Students who chose to take an extension on a visualization viewing day are still expected to be in class that day to provide feedback to classmates. Moreover, arriving without a visualization to show will negatively impact a student's ability to demonstrate competency 10. When a student takes an extension on p1 of a3-6 it is their responsibility to bring the completed visualization to office hours for instructor feedback and to coordinate a critique session with classmates to complete the assignment.

Revise and Resubmit

You may have heard that this class has unlimited revise and resubmits for all work. Under the competency-based grading model there will no longer be unlimited revise and resubmit. Instead, students have multiple opportunities throughout the semester to demonstrate competency in each of the ten course competencies. In addition, there is a revision opportunity built into the semester via a6.

Academic Integrity

Students are strongly encouraged to form study groups and to collaborate on assignments. The following information is required for all submitted work:

  • The names of all collaborating students be listed at the top of the submission. If you worked alone, please state: "I did not collaborate with anyone on this assignment."
  • A "References" section, with in-line citations to any resources you used. Citations should include page numbers (if a printed resource) or a direct URL (if an online resource). If you did not use any resources in completing the assignment, please state: "I did not utilize any external resources in completing this assignment."

You may consult online resources as part of your coursework, but you may not directly copy thoughts or code from online sources.

Generative AI may not be used to perform the intellectual tasks required to complete an assignment. In other words, your writing and code must be your own, not generated by AI (this includes modifying code or text initially generated by ChatGPT - doing so is a violation of academic integrity for this course).

You may use generative AI to help debug your code or as a spell check, provided you submit a PDF of your entire chat transcript with your assignment. Absence of said PDF transcript is a violation of academic integrity for this course.

Comfy Class Policy

Everyone is welcome to make themselves comfortable in our classroom and asked to be respectful of one another. When you are communicating, please practice active listening by focusing on understanding what others are expressing rather than thinking of how you will respond. Additionally, keep in mind that our wide array of individual backgrounds shape our unique perspectives, so please respect one another when we have sincere differences of opinion.

You may bring beverages or snacks, but please use closed containers to avoid spills and keep messy foods away from computers. Everyone is free to use concentration accommodations like fidget toys, knitting, doodling, moving around, or sitting on the floor; just be mindful your focus does not disrupt others. Parents and caregivers may bring their babies and children to class whenever necessary. Learners of all stages are invited to join us.
